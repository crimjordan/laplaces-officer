PUBLIC METHOD SPECIFICATION / CIVILIZATION ENGINE 2.0

AN ENTIRE SOCIETY.
EXPLICIT ASSUMPTIONS.

A public description of how generated territories, cohort-component population flows, constrained governments, housing stocks, program access, crime types, and policy comparisons are computed.

THE MODEL HAS NOT BEEN CALIBRATED OR EXTERNALLY VALIDATED.

The computational architecture incorporates certain concepts and narrow effect anchors supported by research. That does not make the simulated crime rates, population pathways, policy effects, budgets or housing markets empirical estimates. Neither model-generated maps nor 200-year paths should inform actual enforcement decisions.

01 / OBJECT AND SCALE

What exists in Civilization II?

State · 1,320 synthetic areas

9 provinces, six generated city centers, seven land-use classes, up to 200 years.

Country · 2,688 synthetic areas

16 provinces, 12 generated urban clusters, transport corridors and rivers, up to 200 years.

Federation · 4,368 synthetic areas

24 provinces, 19 urban anchors, a maximum of 120 years for performance.

Each cell represents a fictional aggregate geographic area, not an identifiable resident or address. The map is generated with seeded fractal terrain, fictional rivers, highway connections, province centers, and distance-to-city zoning rules. A new seed generates a distinct fictional society. Province and town identifiers are unique and stable within each generated world.

Each cell tracks population by four age groups, housing units, jobs, wages, schooling, access to care, age-specific outcomes, institutional trust, program administration, crime and recording intensity. The visible 3D towers are visualization proxies, not actual buildings. There are no person-level forecasts.

02 / DEMOGRAPHIC ACCOUNTING

Population cannot simply remain the same in every policy world

GenESIS uses an approximate cohort-component structure: children 0–17, young adults 18–34, adults 35–64 and older adults 65+. People enter through births, age out of cohorts, die at assumed age-specific hazards, and move internally or across borders. Internal movements sum to zero over the entire fictional nation.

Population at t + 1
  = Population at t
    + Simulated births
    - Simulated deaths
    + Net external migration

Internal migration across provinces:
  Sum(internal arrivals - departures) = 0 nationally

The engine checks the accounting identity numerically after each simulated year and rejects a time step if it fails by more than floating-point tolerance.

SUPPORTED METHODS · NOT CALIBRATED PARAMETERSUnited Nations, World Population Prospects 2024

Real demographic projections use cohort-component accounting with detailed fertility, mortality, and migration inputs. The UN implementation includes country-specific, age- and sex-specific rates; this four-cohort simulator does not. Official UN methodology.

ACCOUNTING VALIDATIONUK Office for National Statistics — cohort-component framework

The familiar population identity is births minus deaths plus net migration. The model borrows this bookkeeping, not observed British population rates. Methodology.

03 / THE FISCAL STATE

Announced government spending is not the same as feasible spending

GDP is a stylized function of workers, fictional real wages and jobs. Public revenue depends on a tax-rate slider; planned spending is a share of GDP. Debt interest and a borrowing limit restrict appropriations. Six sectors compete for the actually funded public budget. A service provider's delivery depends on local administrative capacity, shocks, and probabilistic implementation failures.

Government revenue = fictional GDP × tax rate
Debt service = previous government debt × assumed interest rate

Available for programs
  = revenue + permitted new borrowing - debt service

Appropriated spending
  = min(target % GDP, available for programs)

Actual delivered services
  = sector allocation × local administrative delivery fraction

Fiscal deficit = appropriations + interest - revenue
Government debt(t+1) = max(0, debt(t) + deficit)

Default assumptions: 2.8% annual interest, public budget shares allocated across education, health, housing, jobs, policing, and climate resilience; implementation efficiency depends on administrative quality and stress. These are fictional numerical parameters. GDP and money are constant-2027-dollar equivalents and do not correspond to a national economy or an IMF baseline.

Changing housing funding may reduce funds available for policing under the same total budget; increasing a tax rate can increase capacity, but there is no calibrated labor-supply or tax-behavioral model. Debt limits have no empirically estimated default risk.

05 / DISTINCT OFFENSE TYPES AND POLICE RECORDING

Recorded incidents are not underlying harm

The engine models theft, burglary, vehicle theft, robbery, assault, homicide, fraud, cybercrime, drug-market offenses and disorder incidents independently. Each category uses a hypothetical annual count baseline and category-specific spatial factors. Population exposure, socioeconomic indexes and simulated shocks can modify event generation. Patrol deterrence is an optional sensitivity assumption.

Simulated underlying event count
  = annual offense rate (invented)
    × exposed population / 1,000
    × modeled geographic/social risk
    × category-specific policy multipliers
    × seeded event variation

Police-recorded event count
  = subset of underlying events
    shaped by reported proportion,
    surveillance, trust and patrol presence

Drug-possession criminal processing is distinct from drug-market offending and from nonfatal overdose. The police-recording model should not be used to claim actual officer detection rates, criminal prevalence, or credible local hot-spots forecasts.

06 / INTERNATIONAL POLICY EVIDENCE

Only narrow outcomes receive numerical study anchors

RANDOMIZED EXPERIMENT / NARROW TRANSFERChicago youth employment — Sara B. Heller (2014)

A randomized trial among selected disadvantaged Chicago high-school youth found a 43% reduction in violent-crime arrests over 16 months for program assignment. This is not a nationwide crime-reduction estimate, an effect on homicide, or proof of permanent employment gains. Civilization applies the related 0.57 ratio only to the simulated offered-youth arrest component. Research article.

OBSERVATIONAL COHORT / ASSOCIATIONMedication after nonfatal overdose — Larochelle et al. (2018)

In a Massachusetts cohort of prior overdose survivors, methadone treatment was associated with a lower adjusted opioid-related death hazard (0.41). Civilizations II uses the associated relative factor for a fictional treated survivor subset, not a randomized or nationally validated treatment effect. The eligibility pool and untreated hazard remain uncalibrated. Study.

META-ANALYSIS / POPULATION LIMITATIONSCorrectional education — RAND (2013)

Correctional education appears related to lower recidivism odds in the studied context. The model uses a 0.57 odds multiplier in a fictional offered subset. The underlying recidivism risk, program completion and jurisdictional transport are assumptions. Research report.

QUALITATIVE POLICY INSPIRATION / NO DIRECT COEFFICIENTFinland, Portugal, Scotland and Norway

Housing First, health-first drug policy, violence reduction and reentry are prompts for exploring institutional design, not empirical estimates of how much crime will change when they are imported to a fictional national environment. Their implementation strengths, delivery rates and interactions are entirely hypothetical. Portugal evidence and limitations.

07 / EXPERIMENTAL DESIGN

What a matched simulation can and cannot answer

World A and B share a common geography and pseudorandom streams. Changing only a single factor isolates its programmed influence under that particular set of equations. Changing several spending, surveillance and national policy controls together creates a package comparison. Repeated seeds in the national experiment tab yield means and 5th–95th percentile ranges of *within-model* differences.

Paired synthetic contrast (seed s)
  = outcome in World B under seed s
    − outcome in World A under seed s

Reported experiment mean
  = average contrast across distinct fictional seeds

Reported 5th–95th percentiles
  = seed-distribution percentiles, NOT
    confidence limits for real-world policies

The national replication workflow starts new fictional histories using current input settings; it does not automatically reconstruct each historical slider movement previously made in the interactive simulator. Neither matched seeds nor explicit government accounting establish external validity.

08 / RESEARCH ROADMAP

What genuine calibration would require

Population foundations

Age-sex single-year census structure, vital rates, migration patterns and cohort-specific exposure times.

Geographic foundations

Actual boundary polygons, land-use parcels, building and housing inventory, transport networks and regional prices.

Public finances

Observed budget accounts, debt and cost data, service quality measures, actual program eligibility and take-up.

Criminal justice

Crime-specific victimization prevalence, incident reporting rates, police contact data, recidivism outcomes and publication of measurement assumptions.

Policy evidence

Clearly defined estimands, identification strategies, study population matching, uncertainty, and checks for transportability between jurisdictions.

Validation

Historical holdout tests, sensitivity to omitted mechanisms, seed replication, calibration targets, and independent benchmarks.
