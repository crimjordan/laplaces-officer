THE SCIENCE
BEHIND THE FICTION.

Model architecture, mathematical assumptions, geographic generation, international policy inspirations, research anchors, and boundaries of valid interpretation.

THIS IS NOT AN EMPIRICALLY CALIBRATED NATIONAL FORECAST. SOVEREIGN is a fictional experimental geography simulator. Its numerical crime, mortality, migration, schooling, housing, surveillance and economic parameters are mostly invented. No region represents a real community, and no policy output should be used to decide where police operate or to predict criminal behavior.
01 / THE COMPUTATIONAL OBJECT

What the simulator actually generates

State: 1,320 cells

44 × 30 grid, 9 fictional provinces, 6 procedurally placed city anchors, 200-year maximum horizon.

Country: 2,688 cells

64 × 42 grid, 16 fictional provinces, 12 city anchors, 200-year maximum horizon.

Federation: 4,368 cells

84 × 52 grid, 24 fictional provinces, 19 city anchors, 120-year maximum horizon for performance.

Each cell has a synthetic population, employment, education, housing stability, health access, trust index, deprivation, development, police-recording score and drug-overdose survivor pool. People are represented as aggregate counts, not individual agents. Unlike the city-scale GENESIS simulator, SOVEREIGN does not model separate named resident lives.

Each world evolves in annual steps. World A and World B begin with identical spatial data and deterministic random streams. Changing a policy may alter later state transitions and measured outcomes. Annual typed-array snapshots enable historical inspection and timeline branching. Repeated independent seed experiments would be required to measure within-model stochastic variability.

02 / PROCEDURALLY GENERATED GEOGRAPHY

Why the territory looks different in every seed

The map is not sourced from OpenStreetMap, satellite imagery or census data. It is produced from seeded multiscale spatial noise, fictitious city centers, nonlinear waterways, connected road lines and jittered province centers. Urbanicity results from proximity to generated cities and transport, as well as surface terrain. Distinct land uses include urban residential, suburban, commercial/CBD, industrial, rural/agricultural, woodland/wilderness and water/river.

Land-use type, density, terrain index, employment opportunity and road access influence hypothetical regional conditions. A stylized transport animation does not depict real vehicle routes. Building extrusions represent generated development indexes, not actual infrastructure.

Illustrative event-generation logic

Reference expected incidents
  = synthetic population / 1000
    × hypothetical offense baseline
    × regional deprivation/urbanicity factor
    × neighbor-spillover adjustment

Final expected incidents
  = reference expectation
    × category-specific scenario adjustments
    × any applicable policy exposure multiplier
    × stylized patrol modifier

Realized incidents
  = nonnegative rounded expectation
    + seed-stable event noise

Recorded incidents
  = subset of realized incidents
    shaped by reporting probability,
      surveillance, trust and patrol presence

These are structural equations chosen for explanatory interactivity, not a model estimated by regression, trial analysis or historical time-series fitting. The deterministic noise makes identical worlds reproducible but does not estimate causal effects.

04 / INTERNATIONAL POLICY EXCHANGE

Program ideas are not transportable effect estimates

Finland · Housing FirstDescriptive policy experience / aggregate homelessness trends

The software assumes sustained housing support improves the fictional housing-stability index at an invented rate. Finland's declining long-term homelessness does not identify a universal causal crime effect.

European Parliament housing research
Portugal · Health-first drug policyPolicy-history evaluations with multiple co-occurring changes

Modeled decriminalization changes criminal processing of possession contacts and referrals, not drug-market activity or overdose events automatically. Treatment expansion, service capacity and legislation co-occur historically.

UNSW Drug Policy Modelling Program (2024)
Scotland · Violence reduction / focused deterrenceInspirational framework; review evidence largely quasi-experimental

The illustrative intervention adjusts some violent-incident expectations with an invented coefficient, not a coefficient imported from Scottish evaluations. The systematic review found encouraging but context-dependent associations.

Braga, Weisburd & Turchan — systematic review
Norway · Reentry and correctional educationNorway-inspired scenario; numerical anchor from US RAND meta-analysis

Correctional education is an international reentry-policy inspiration. The recidivism odds ratio 0.57 comes from a US-centered correctional-education meta-analysis, not from a Norwegian treatment study. The simulator's baseline and program take-up are hypothetical.

RAND correctional education research
United States / Chicago · Summer youth jobsRandomized trial; narrow specific outcome

Heller's trial reported a 43% reduction in violent-crime arrests over 16 months among assigned selected disadvantaged youth. This simulator applies a 0.57 multiplier only to the fictional subset offered youth jobs. It does not reduce all underlying crime or predict future adult employment.

Heller (2014), Science
Cross-context medication treatment · Opioid-overdose mortalityObservational Massachusetts cohort, not a country-level experiment

Larochelle et al. reported an adjusted opioid-overdose mortality hazard ratio of 0.41 during methadone treatment among survivors of prior nonfatal overdose. The simulator uses this narrow conditional multiplier. The baseline survivor pool, annual hazard and service capacity are fictional.

Larochelle et al. (2018), Annals of Internal Medicine

Integrated policy exchange is a deliberately synthetic combination of several inspirations. No evidence establishes its assumed uptake, interaction effects, or superiority over a single policy.

05 / HOW TO RUN A VALID MODEL COMPARISON

Methods for interpreting the virtual society

Match conditions

Use the same geographic seed, budgets, shocks and province targeting, while changing only a defined policy factor where possible.

Compare specific outcomes

Do not treat arrests, underlying victimizations, police recordings, recidivism and overdose mortality as interchangeable.

Inspect mechanisms

Compare housing support, treatment starts, health referrals, youth offers and migration as intermediate modeled outcomes.

Reproduce the scenario

Export national annual CSV and JSON describing region generation, policies, shocks and yearly results.

The long horizon is particularly speculative. A 100- or 200-year output is a demonstration of compounding structural assumptions, not a credible social forecast. The model currently lacks fiscal budget constraints, price systems, climate models, validated cohorts, demographic age transitions, parameter uncertainty, and external evaluation. A convincing national research instrument would require those elements and real calibration data.
